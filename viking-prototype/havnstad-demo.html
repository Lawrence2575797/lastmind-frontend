Economics · 1.1 Nature of economics — real LastMind lesson, explanation only Building on unlimited wants and finite resources, scarcity is the fundamental economic problem: because resources are limited but human wants are infinite, there is never enough to satisfy everyone's wants fully.

This creates a permanent gap between what people would like to have and what the available resources can actually produce. Scarcity is universal — it affects every economy and every individual, regardless of how rich or developed they are.

Because of scarcity, choices must be made about what to produce, how to produce it, and who receives it. These choices are unavoidable precisely because resources cannot stretch to cover every want simultaneously.
Economics · 1.2 How markets work — real LastMind lesson, explanation only Building on the definition of demand, the law of demand states that as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded of it falls, and as price falls, quantity demanded rises — holding all other factors constant (ceteris paribus).

This is an inverse relationship: price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions. Plotted on a graph with price on the vertical axis and quantity demanded on the horizontal axis, this relationship produces a downward-sloping demand curve.

The 'ceteris paribus' condition matters: the law only holds when factors like income, tastes, and prices of other goods stay fixed, isolating the effect of price alone on quantity demanded.
Economics · 1.2 How markets work — real LastMind lesson, explanation only Building on drawing demand and supply curves, plotting both on the same diagram shows they cross at one point.

Equilibrium price is the price at which the quantity demanded exactly equals the quantity supplied — where the demand and supply curves intersect. Equilibrium quantity is the amount bought and sold at that price.

At this point, there is no tendency for price to change: buyers are willing to buy exactly what sellers are willing to sell, so the market clears with nothing left over and no shortage.
